# Difficulty Rubric

This file defines the three difficulty tiers used across all PE Content Generator outputs.
Match the requested difficulty to these definitions before generating content.

---

## Tier 1 — Recall

**Bloom's Level:** Remember / Understand
**Cognitive demand:** The student retrieves a fact, definition, or formula and applies it directly with minimal transformation.
**FE alignment:** Common on FE exams for foundational topics.
**PE alignment:** Rarely appears at PE level; used only for warm-up or prerequisite checks.

**Characteristics:**
- Single-step solution or direct formula plug-in
- All given values are provided; no extraction from diagrams or tables required
- No unit conversion required (or a single trivial conversion)
- One concept per problem; no cross-topic synthesis
- Distractor answers target formula misremembering or unit errors

**Example signal phrases:** "What is the definition of...", "Which formula governs...", "Calculate X given Y and Z directly"

---

## Tier 2 — Application

**Bloom's Level:** Apply
**Cognitive demand:** The student selects the correct method from multiple candidates and executes a multi-step solution, interpreting given conditions to choose the right approach.
**FE alignment:** Core FE difficulty. Most exam problems live here.
**PE alignment:** Entry-level PE problems; common in breadth sections.

**Characteristics:**
- Two to four solution steps
- Requires selecting the correct formula from a family (e.g., which deflection equation applies)
- May require unit conversion or diagram reading
- One or two boundary conditions or constraints to apply
- Distractor answers target sign errors, wrong formula selection, or missing conversion factors

**Example signal phrases:** "A simply supported beam with..., find the deflection at midspan", "Given this flow rate and pipe diameter, determine the head loss"

---

## Tier 3 — Analysis

**Bloom's Level:** Analyze / Evaluate
**Cognitive demand:** The student decomposes a complex scenario into components, applies multiple concepts in sequence, and interprets results — often including a judgment call or design check.
**FE alignment:** End-of-exam challenge problems; not always present.
**PE alignment:** Core PE difficulty. Most depth exam problems live here.

**Characteristics:**
- Four or more solution steps
- Cross-topic synthesis required (e.g., fluid mechanics + energy balance)
- Must extract and interpret data from tables, charts, or multi-part diagrams
- Includes at least one judgment step (e.g., "check if this design meets code", "which scenario governs")
- Distractor answers include plausible-but-wrong intermediate results that a student could reach by stopping too early

**Example signal phrases:** "Determine whether the proposed design satisfies ASCE 7 load requirements", "A two-phase system operating at steady state — find the mass flow rate and verify the energy balance"

---

## Borderline Guidance

If a problem sits between tiers:
- **Recall vs. Application:** Does the student need to *choose* the method, or just execute one? If choosing, it's Application.
- **Application vs. Analysis:** Does the student need to synthesize across more than one concept domain, or make a judgment call? If yes, it's Analysis.

When in doubt, round up to the harder tier and note it.
