# Worked Example Format

Worked examples are fully narrated solutions. Unlike practice problems, which ask the student to solve,
worked examples *show* how an expert thinks through a problem. The voice is instructional — like a
tutor explaining at a whiteboard, not a textbook presenting a proof.

---

## Worked Example Template

```
WORKED EXAMPLE: [TOPIC]
Exam Level: [FE / PE] | Discipline: [Discipline] | Difficulty: [Tier]
Bloom's Level: [Apply / Analyze]

---

THE PROBLEM

[Same format as practice problem stem: scenario, given values, find statement.]

---

BEFORE WE SOLVE

[2–3 sentences identifying the concept at play, naming the governing equation or method,
and flagging any conditions or constraints the student must notice before starting.
This is the "expert noticing" step — model what an experienced engineer sees first.]

---

STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION

**Step 1 — [Name the step]**
[Explain *why* this step comes first. Then show the equation before substitution.]

Equation:
[Governing formula with all variables labeled]

Substitution:
[Plug in values with units, one line at a time]

Result: [value + unit]

[Repeat for each step. Every step has a name, a why, an equation, a substitution, and a result.]

---

**Step [N] — Final Answer**
[State the final answer clearly with value and unit.]
[One sentence interpreting what the answer means in context of the problem.]

Final Answer: [value + unit]

---

WHAT COULD GO WRONG

[Name 1–2 specific errors a student could make on this type of problem and show what answer
they would get if they made that error. This is the misconception integration step.]

Error 1: [Name the error]
- A student who [makes this mistake] would get [wrong value + unit]
- Why it's wrong: [Brief explanation]

---

EXAM TAKEAWAY

[One to two sentences the student should commit to memory from this example.
Should be a transferable rule, not just a restatement of the answer.]

⚠ SPEC FLAG (if applicable)
[Note any NCEES specification change post-2024 that affects this content.]
```

---

## Narration Guidelines

**Do** narrate the reasoning, not just the math.
- ✓ "We use Bernoulli's equation here because the flow is steady, incompressible, and along a streamline."
- ✗ "Apply Bernoulli's equation."

**Do** name the decision points.
- ✓ "Before choosing which moment of inertia formula to use, we need to identify the axis of bending."
- ✗ "Calculate the moment of inertia."

**Do not** pad steps with unnecessary text. If the step is mechanical (e.g., unit conversion), execute
it concisely without a lengthy rationale.

**Do not** editorialize on difficulty.
- ✗ "This is a tricky one!" or "This is straightforward."
- ✓ Just solve it clearly.
