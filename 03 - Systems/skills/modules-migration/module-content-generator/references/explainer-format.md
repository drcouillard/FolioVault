# Explainer Format

Concept explainers teach a topic from first principles to exam application. They are not reference pages —
they guide the student through understanding, not just information. The structure moves from anchor
(what the concept is) to mechanism (why it works) to application (how to use it on the exam).

---

## Explainer Template

```
CONCEPT EXPLAINER: [TOPIC NAME]
Exam Level: [FE / PE] | Discipline: [Discipline]
Prereqs: [List any concepts the student should already understand before reading this]

---

THE CORE IDEA

[2–3 sentences stating the concept in plain language, without jargon, before introducing any formulas.
Use an analogy if the concept has one. This is not a dictionary definition — it's a mental model.]

Analogy (if applicable): [Plain-language comparison to an everyday physical experience]

---

THE FORMAL DEFINITION

[Now introduce the precise engineering definition.]
[Define all key terms introduced in the definition.]

---

GOVERNING EQUATION(S)

[Present the core formula(s). For each equation:]

[Equation]

Where:
- [Variable] = [what it represents] ([SI unit] / [USCS unit if exam-relevant])
- [Repeat for each variable]

Assumptions: [State when this formula applies and when it does not]

---

HOW TO USE IT ON THE EXAM

[Numbered list of the decision steps a student follows to apply this concept on an exam problem.
Be specific. Name what to look for in the problem stem, what to check before applying the formula,
and what units to verify.]

1. [First decision or check]
2. [Second step]
3. [Apply the formula / solve]
4. [Verify or interpret the result]

---

COMMON MISTAKES

[Name 2–3 specific misconceptions students have about this concept.
For each, explain why it's wrong and what the correct understanding is.]

Mistake 1: [State the misconception]
- Why students think this: [Brief explanation]
- What's actually true: [Correct understanding]

Mistake 2: ...

---

QUICK EXAMPLE

[A single Tier 1 or Tier 2 problem (no full template required — just setup + answer) that
illustrates the concept. No more than 5 lines. The goal is to ground the explainer in numbers,
not to teach problem-solving procedure (that belongs in a worked example).]

Example:
[Scenario in one sentence. Given values. Find.]
Solution: [Equation → result with units]

---

EXAM SUMMARY

[Bullet list of 3–5 things the student must know cold about this concept for the exam.
Facts, not fluff. These should be flashcard-worthy.]

- [Key fact 1]
- [Key fact 2]
- [Key fact 3]

⚠ SPEC FLAG (if applicable)
[Note any NCEES specification change post-2024 affecting this concept.]
```

---

## Explainer Length Guidelines

| Concept complexity | Target length |
|---|---|
| Single formula, narrow application | 300–500 words |
| Multi-formula or condition-dependent | 500–800 words |
| Cross-topic or code-dependent concept | 800–1100 words |

Do not exceed 1100 words. If a topic demands more, split into two explainers (foundational + advanced).

## Analogy Bank (use these sparingly — only when they genuinely clarify)

- Stress and strain → rubber band stretching under force
- Hydraulic head → water pressure as height of water above a reference point
- Resonance → pushing a child on a swing at the natural frequency
- Heat transfer by conduction → touching a cold metal railing vs. a cold wooden door
- Factor of safety → building a bridge rated for 10 tons when max expected load is 5 tons

Do not force analogies. If one does not fit naturally, skip it.
