# Voice Framework — Blueprint

Used in Step 2 of brand-voice-system. Load when defining tone position for a brand.

---

## The Four Tone Axes

Every brand sits somewhere on each of these four axes. The position is derived from archetype and emotional keywords — not preference.

Mark each axis with a specific position (not just "middle") and a one-sentence justification.

---

### Axis 1: Formal ↔ Casual

```
Formal ————————————————————————— Casual
  1    2    3    4    5    6    7
```

- **1–2 (Formal):** Full sentences, no contractions, elevated vocabulary. Archetypes: Ruler, Sage.
- **3–4 (Professional):** Mostly complete sentences, occasional contractions, neutral vocabulary. Archetypes: Hero, Explorer, Caregiver.
- **5–6 (Conversational):** Contractions normal, sentence fragments acceptable, accessible vocabulary. Archetypes: Everyman, Innocent, Creator.
- **7 (Casual):** Fragments, slang, deliberate informality. Archetypes: Jester, Outlaw.

**Decision rule:** If the audience profile includes executive buyers or high-stakes purchases, skew formal by 1–2 points regardless of archetype.

---

### Axis 2: Serious ↔ Playful

```
Serious ————————————————————————— Playful
  1    2    3    4    5    6    7
```

- **1–2 (Serious):** No humor, all weight. Every word earns its place. Archetypes: Sage, Ruler.
- **3–4 (Grounded):** Occasionally warm, never silly. Levity used sparingly and purposefully. Archetypes: Hero, Caregiver, Explorer.
- **5–6 (Light):** Humor welcome, wit preferred over slapstick. Archetypes: Creator, Everyman, Innocent.
- **7 (Playful):** Humor is a primary tool. Puns, wordplay, irreverence. Archetypes: Jester, Outlaw.

**Decision rule:** Anti-positioning often catches brands that want to be playful but shouldn't be. Check before placing above 5.

---

### Axis 3: Authoritative ↔ Humble

```
Authoritative ————————————————— Humble
  1    2    3    4    5    6    7
```

- **1–2 (Authoritative):** Confident statements, no hedging, positions are declared. Archetypes: Ruler, Sage, Hero.
- **3–4 (Confident):** Strong opinions held with openness. Archetypes: Creator, Explorer.
- **5–6 (Collaborative):** "We think," "we believe," invites the reader in. Archetypes: Caregiver, Everyman, Innocent.
- **7 (Self-deprecating):** Openly acknowledges limits, pokes fun at itself. Archetypes: Jester, Outlaw (sometimes).

**Decision rule:** Authoritative voice requires credibility to back it up. If the brand is new or the audience is skeptical, humble is safer — overconfidence without proof alienates.

---

### Axis 4: Expressive ↔ Reserved

```
Expressive ————————————————————— Reserved
  1    2    3    4    5    6    7
```

- **1–2 (Expressive):** Bold language, metaphor, emotional color, sensory detail. Archetypes: Lover, Magician, Jester.
- **3–4 (Characterized):** Has a clear personality without theatrics. Archetypes: Creator, Explorer, Outlaw.
- **5–6 (Neutral):** Lets the product do the talking. Clean, efficient, minimal personality. Archetypes: Sage, Everyman.
- **7 (Reserved):** Near-invisible voice. Maximum clarity, minimum personality. Archetypes: Ruler (in some executions).

| Outlaw | 6–7 | 5–6 | 4–5 | 2–3 |

---

## Tone Shift Rules (Context Sensitivity)

The brand voice is consistent. The tone adjusts by context. Document these shifts explicitly.

| Context | Adjustment from Baseline |
|---|---|
| Error messages / bad news | +1 toward Humble, +1 toward Serious |
| Onboarding / welcome | +1 toward Warm/Playful |
| Legal / compliance | +2 toward Formal, voice still present in framing |
| Crisis communication | Serious locked at 1–2, all other axes toward neutral |
| Social media | +1 toward Casual, +1 toward Expressive (within brand range) |
| Sales / CTA moments | +1 toward Authoritative, shorter sentences |

---

## Tone Description Format

After placing the brand on all four axes, write a single paragraph that describes the voice. Write it *in* that voice — the paragraph itself is the proof of concept.

Example (Explorer/Everyman, position ~4/3/3/3):

> "Fieldwork doesn't lecture. We write like the person who's been out there — who knows the gear, trusts the process, and has no patience for fluff. Clear sentences. Direct opinions. Occasionally a sentence that just breathes. We earn credibility by being useful, not impressive."

If the paragraph sounds wrong, the axis positions are wrong. Adjust and rewrite before moving on.
