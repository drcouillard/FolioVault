# Brand Archetypes Reference

Based on the 12-archetype framework (Carl Jung / Margaret Mark & Carol S. Pearson).
Use this file to select and justify the archetype in Step 3 of the Brand Identity Strategy workflow.

---

## How to Select

1. Match the brand's **core motivation** to the archetype's driver
2. Verify the **communication style** aligns with intake Block B answers
3. Check the **danger zone** — if the brand is already drifting there, note it
4. Confirm with the user before locking

A brand can have a **primary** and a **secondary** archetype. Secondary adds nuance — don't use it to avoid committing to the primary.

---

## The 12 Archetypes

### 1. The Innocent
- **Driver:** Safety, happiness, simplicity
- **Promise:** Things will be okay
- **Communication style:** Warm, optimistic, honest, uncomplicated
- **Typography tendency:** Rounded, clean, approachable serifs or soft sans-serifs
- **Color tendency:** Light, pure tones — white, pastels, sky blue, natural greens
- **Examples:** Dove, Coca-Cola (classic), Aveeno
- **Danger zone:** Naïve, out of touch, too soft to be credible

---

### 2. The Explorer
- **Driver:** Freedom, discovery, authenticity
- **Promise:** Don't fence me in
- **Communication style:** Bold, adventurous, direct, anti-establishment
- **Typography tendency:** Rugged, utilitarian — strong sans-serifs, slab serifs
- **Color tendency:** Earth tones, deep greens, burnt orange, sky blue
- **Examples:** Patagonia, Jeep, REI
- **Danger zone:** Aimless, uncommitted, all wandering and no purpose

---

### 3. The Sage
- **Driver:** Truth, knowledge, wisdom
- **Promise:** The truth will set you free
- **Communication style:** Authoritative, measured, informative, credible
- **Typography tendency:** Classic serifs, editorial — high readability, structured
- **Color tendency:** Navy, deep blue, forest green, stone gray
- **Examples:** Google, TED, The Economist, McKinsey
- **Danger zone:** Elitist, cold, condescending, paralyzed by analysis

---

### 4. The Hero
- **Driver:** Mastery, achievement, making the world better through action
- **Promise:** Where there's a will, there's a way
- **Communication style:** Confident, motivating, direct, competence-first
- **Typography tendency:** Bold, strong, modern sans-serifs — clean and powerful
- **Color tendency:** Black, red, navy, gold — high-contrast, commanding
- **Examples:** Nike, BMW, Duracell, FedEx
- **Danger zone:** Arrogant, preachy, sets up expectations it can't meet

---

### 5. The Outlaw
- **Driver:** Revolution, disruption, breaking rules that don't serve people
- **Promise:** Rules are made to be broken
- **Communication style:** Raw, provocative, anti-mainstream, unapologetic
- **Typography tendency:** Distressed, hand-lettered, or aggressively bold type
- **Color tendency:** Black, red, high-contrast — sometimes deliberately ugly
- **Examples:** Harley-Davidson, Diesel, Oatly
- **Danger zone:** Shock for shock's sake, alienates rather than rallies

---

### 6. The Magician
- **Driver:** Transformation, making dreams real
- **Promise:** It can happen
- **Communication style:** Visionary, inspiring, a little mysterious, possibility-forward
- **Typography tendency:** Distinctive, sometimes unexpected — custom type, elegant and dramatic
- **Color tendency:** Purple, midnight blue, gold, black — sense of wonder and power
- **Examples:** Apple, Disney, Dyson
- **Danger zone:** Deceptive, overpromising, style over substance

---

### 7. The Regular Guy/Gal (Everyman)
- **Driver:** Belonging, being real, connecting with others
- **Promise:** All men and women are created equal
- **Communication style:** Unpretentious, friendly, practical, relatable
- **Typography tendency:** No-fuss, readable — functional sans-serifs, nothing fancy
- **Color tendency:** Earth tones, practical mid-tones, nothing flashy
- **Examples:** IKEA, Levi's, Home Depot
- **Danger zone:** Boring, invisible, too generic to be remembered

---

### 8. The Lover
- **Driver:** Intimacy, passion, beauty, connection
- **Promise:** You deserve to feel special
- **Communication style:** Sensuous, warm, inviting, emotionally rich
- **Typography tendency:** Elegant serifs, flowing scripts, refined and beautiful
- **Color tendency:** Deep reds, rose, burgundy, gold, rich jewel tones
- **Examples:** Chanel, Victoria's Secret, Godiva, Häagen-Dazs
- **Danger zone:** Superficial, manipulative, too focused on image

---

### 9. The Jester
- **Driver:** Joy, humor, living in the moment
- **Promise:** Life's too short to be serious
- **Communication style:** Playful, irreverent, witty, self-aware
- **Typography tendency:** Playful, quirky — irregular, bright, sometimes deliberately off
- **Color tendency:** Bright, saturated, high-energy — yellows, oranges, unexpected combos
- **Examples:** Old Spice, Dollar Shave Club, Skittles, Wendy's (social)
- **Danger zone:** Flippant at the wrong moment, not taken seriously when it matters

---

### 10. The Caregiver
- **Driver:** Service, protection, nurturing others
- **Promise:** I'll take care of you
- **Communication style:** Empathetic, warm, supportive, selfless
- **Typography tendency:** Friendly, readable — nothing sharp or aggressive
- **Color tendency:** Warm whites, gentle blues, greens, soft yellows
- **Examples:** Johnson & Johnson, UNICEF, Whole Foods
- **Danger zone:** Martyrdom, enabling, coming off as patronizing

---

### 11. The Creator
- **Driver:** Innovation, self-expression, building things of enduring value
- **Promise:** If you can imagine it, it can be created
- **Communication style:** Expressive, original, process-forward, craft-focused
- **Typography tendency:** Distinctive, often custom — celebrates the unusual
- **Color tendency:** Broad range — often brand-defining rather than category-typical
- **Examples:** LEGO, Adobe, Crayola, Pinterest
- **Danger zone:** Perfectionism, impractical, too niche to scale

---

### 12. The Ruler
- **Driver:** Control, order, leadership, building prosperous community
- **Promise:** Power isn't everything — it's the only thing
- **Communication style:** Authoritative, polished, commanding, exclusive
- **Typography tendency:** Formal, structured — classic serifs, refined hierarchy
- **Color tendency:** Black, gold, deep navy, white — premium, restrained
- **Examples:** Mercedes-Benz, Rolex, American Express, Microsoft
- **Danger zone:** Elitist, rigid, loses touch with real customer experience

---

## Quick Pairing Guide

When a brand sits between two archetypes, common pairings:
- **Hero + Outlaw** → Challenger brand with a cause (Under Armour energy)
- **Sage + Ruler** → Expert authority brand (consulting, law, finance)
- **Creator + Magician** → Innovation-led design brand
- **Caregiver + Innocent** → Wellness, children's, or natural goods
- **Explorer + Outlaw** → Anti-mainstream adventure (outdoors, travel)
- **Jester + Everyman** → Mass-market challenger with personality
