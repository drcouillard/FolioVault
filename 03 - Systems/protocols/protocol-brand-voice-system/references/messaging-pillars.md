# Messaging Pillars — Blueprint

Used in Step 5 of brand-voice-system. Load when building messaging pillars for a brand.

---

## What Messaging Pillars Are

Pillars are the 3–5 core themes a brand always returns to. They are not slogans. They are not values statements. They are the recurring arguments the brand makes — the things it keeps saying because they keep being true and relevant.

Every piece of content should touch at least one pillar. If content can't be linked to a pillar, it probably shouldn't be published.

---

## How to Derive Pillars

Pillars come from the intersection of:
1. **What the brand genuinely delivers** (value proposition)
2. **What the audience actually cares about** (audience profiles)
3. **What competitors aren't saying** (competitive differentiation)

A pillar is not invented — it is discovered in the strategy file. If a pillar has no root in the strategy, remove it.

---

## Pillar Architecture

Each pillar has four components:

### 1. Pillar Name
Short, memorable, internal-use label. 2–4 words. Not a headline — a category name.

Examples: "No Fluff," "Built to Last," "The Full Picture," "On Your Terms"

### 2. The Belief (Internal)
What the brand actually believes that produces this pillar. Written as a conviction, not a feature.

Format: "We believe [X]. That's why [Y]."

Example (Fieldwork Coffee Co., "No Fluff" pillar):
> "We believe great coffee doesn't need a mythology around it. That's why we never write tasting notes that sound like a wine auction."

### 3. The Expression (External)
How this belief shows up in actual copy. Not a full campaign — the recurring themes, phrases, and angles the pillar generates.

Format: List of 3–5 ways this pillar appears in content.

### 4. Sample Proof Point
One headline, tagline, or body sentence that demonstrates the pillar in action. Should feel immediately on-brand.

---

## Number of Pillars

- **Minimum: 3** — fewer and the brand has no range; every piece of content sounds the same
- **Maximum: 5** — more and there's no focus; the brand says everything and owns nothing
- **Recommended: 3–4** for most brands

If the user wants more than 5, push back. Ask which ones couldn't be combined or cut.

---

## Pillar Relationships

Pillars should be distinct but complementary. Test:
- Do any two pillars say the same thing? Merge them.
- Does any pillar contradict another? Resolve it at the strategy level.
- Can all pillars exist simultaneously in a single piece of content? They should be able to.

## Common Pillar Mistakes

**Too broad:** "Quality" is not a pillar. Every brand claims quality. Make it specific — what kind of quality, proven how, for whom?

**Too internal:** "We care about our employees" is a belief, not a messaging pillar. Pillars must be audience-relevant.

**Overlapping:** "Transparency" and "Honesty" as separate pillars is redundant. Combine or sharpen.

**Disconnected from strategy:** If you can't point to a line in the strategy file that generated this pillar, it's borrowed from somewhere else. Cut it.
