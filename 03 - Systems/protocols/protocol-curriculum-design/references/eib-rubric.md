# Explain-It-Back (EIB) Rubric — Full Specification

## Purpose

The EIB task develops tutor-acumen — the learner's ability to explain,
demonstrate, and correct errors in others. It is the primary mechanism for
the dual-outcome model: every learner should leave the course capable of
tutoring a peer in the same subject.

---

## Phase-by-Phase Implementation

### Phase 1 — Seed (no evaluation)

Display at end of module after formative assessment:

> "Imagine a peer looks at [core concept from this module] and says:
> '[common misconception].' Could you explain what's wrong and how to
> fix it? You don't need to submit anything yet — just think about how
> you'd explain it. In a few weeks, you'll be asked to write exactly
> this kind of explanation."

Purpose: Plants the tutor frame early. No scoring. No submission.

---

### Phase 2–3 — Evaluated EIB (7-point rubric)

Present a simulated peer scenario. The peer has made a specific, nameable
error — not a vague confusion. The learner writes a correction.

**Scenario format:**
> "A peer submits the following work: [show peer's incorrect work].
> They say: '[peer's reasoning in their own words].'
> Write a clear explanation of what went wrong and how to fix it.
> Include the correct setup and final answer."

**Rubric:**

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | Incorrect or missing | Partially correct | Fully correct content |
| Clarity | Incomprehensible to a peer | Followable with effort | Clear and self-contained |
| Misconception addressed | Error not identified | Error named but not corrected | Error named AND corrected with reasoning |
| Example used | — | Example present | — |

Note: "Example used" is 0 or 1 point only. Maximum = 7 points.
Pass threshold: 5/7 points.

**Misconception selection by Bloom boundary:**

| Transition | Error type to target |
|---|---|
| Phase 1 → 2 (Remember → Apply) | Procedural errors: wrong formula, skipped step, sign error |
| Phase 2 → 3 (Apply → Analyze) | Conceptual errors: correct procedure, wrong understanding of why |
| Phase 3 → 4 (Analyze → Create) | Design/judgment errors: incomplete constraints, unexamined assumptions |

---

### Phase 4 — Teach-Back Capstone

The learner produces a complete instructional artifact, not just a correction.

**Required components:**
1. Clear explanation of the module-level concept (≥2 paragraphs)
2. One fully worked example of their own construction
3. One practice problem (with full solution) a peer could attempt

**Capstone rubric (15 points):**

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Conceptual accuracy | 0–4 |
| Clarity and organization | 0–3 |
| Worked example quality | 0–3 |
| Practice problem appropriateness (correct difficulty, solvable) | 0–3 |
| Misconception anticipation (explanation preempts a common error) | 0–2 |

Pass threshold: 11/15 points.

---

## Feedback on EIB Failure

When a learner scores below threshold:

- Score 4/7 or below: Surface the specific criterion that failed. Require
  one targeted revision before the module advances.
- Score 3/7 or below: Route to a guided EIB scaffold — the learner completes
  a sentence-completion version of the explanation before attempting free-form.

Never simply say "try again" — always identify the specific gap.

---

## Portfolio Eligibility

Phase 2–3 EIB responses scoring 7/7 are eligible for course portfolio.
All Phase 4 Teach-Back Capstones that pass are automatically portfolio-eligible.
Tag each portfolio item with: module ID, concept, Bloom level demonstrated.
