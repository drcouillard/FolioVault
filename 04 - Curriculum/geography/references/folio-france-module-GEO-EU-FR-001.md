## PART B — Instructional Design Foundation

### Enduring Understanding
*(Pillar 1 — written before any content)*

France's geographic position at Western Europe's crossroads — between Atlantic and
Mediterranean, between sea and Alps — has shaped its role as a cultural exporter,
agricultural powerhouse, and political architect of the modern European order.
Geography doesn't determine history, but France is a clear case where it heavily
pressures it.

### Acceptable Evidence — What mastery looks like

A student can explain how France's physical geography (climate zones, river systems,
coastal access, Alpine border) has shaped at least two of the following: agricultural
output, trade relationships, internal migration patterns, or its political relationship
with neighboring states — using specific geographic evidence, not generalization.

### Content Type Classification *(Smith & Ragan)*

**Dominant type: Conceptual-Principle** — knowing why relationships and systems work
as they do. Students must understand the mechanisms connecting France's geographic
position to its global role, not just name features or recite facts.

Secondary type: Declarative — key facts, borders, data required to support analysis.

### Section 5 — Cultural Geography [ADDED]

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Language Geography | French unified the country and became the instrument of both republican identity and colonial reach. The Académie française (est. 1635) regulates the language — an unusual state-backed linguistic institution with no close parallel in other major democracies. Regional languages (Breton, Basque, Alsatian, Occitan, Corsican) survive at the margins; all are recognized but none are co-official. French language policy has been a political flashpoint — the Toubon Law (1994) mandates French in public life and was partly a response to perceived English encroachment. [VERIFY: Toubon Law details] |
| Religious Geography | Laïcité — France's constitutional principle of strict secularism — is not simply the absence of religion but a specific political philosophy separating the state from all religious authority. Historically Catholic; the 1905 Law of Separation of Church and State formalized the break. Today, France has the largest Muslim population in Western Europe (estimated 5–6 million [VERIFY]), concentrated in urban banlieues, postwar housing estates built for labor migrants. Religious identity has become one of France's most contested political geographies. |
| Cultural Geographic Feature | The Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée (AOC) system — France's geographic wine (and cheese and other food) classification system — legally binds product identity to specific territories. Bordeaux, Burgundy, Champagne, Alsace, Loire Valley are not just agricultural zones; they are legally defined geographic appellations where terrain, climate, and historical practice are formally intertwined. A wine cannot be labeled "Champagne" unless it is grown, produced, and bottled within a specific mapped boundary in northeastern France. This is geography encoded into law, with economic and cultural consequences that extend globally. [VERIFY: AOC explanation] |
| Land Use & Cultural Identity | France retains a strong rural identity despite ~81% urbanization — the peasant-farmer archetype (paysan) remains a powerful cultural and political force. Agricultural policy receives disproportionate political attention (France is the EU's largest recipient of Common Agricultural Policy subsidies [VERIFY]). The landscape itself is shaped by centuries of agricultural logic: the bocage (hedgerow) of Normandy, the open field strips of the north, the terraced vineyards of Burgundy. In France, land use is cultural identity. [VERIFY] |

## PART D — Voice & Presentation (continued)

### Wonder Hook
*(ARCS: Attention · Gagné Event 1)*

France is roughly the size of Texas — and yet it shares a land border with eight
countries, touches two seas, contains three distinct mountain ranges, and produces
more distinct regional cuisines than most continents. Notice what that list is
actually telling you: geography isn't just about size.

**Relevance Hook** *(ARCS: Relevance)*

France is the world's most visited country — over 89 million international arrivals
annually. [VERIFY: tourism statistic, current year] Understanding why requires
understanding where it sits, what it produces, and how its geography made it central
to European history for 1,500 years. This isn't history for its own sake — it's a
case study in how place shapes power.

### Big Idea Callback — Passive Instance
*(Mid-module — Folio answers)*

**What's the big idea here?** France is roughly the size of Texas — and yet it shapes
global trade, culture, and security policy in ways Texas doesn't. The difference isn't
just population or history. It's position: where France sits, what it borders, and
how its geography gave it options that most countries of its size never had.

*Decision rule applied:* Concept has no single procedural worked example → analogy
used (Texas size comparison). Worked example to be developed for AOC system and
climate zone application at question level — see Gagné Event 5 note.

### Big Idea Callback — Active EIB Prompt
*(End of module — student answers)*

**What's the big idea here?** You've been asked to brief a group of visiting students
on why France matters globally — and you have two minutes and no maps.

Walk through it:
1. Name two ways France's geography has shaped its global role — be specific.
2. Identify one thing about France that surprised you — and connect it back to
   geography.
3. If France were landlocked and bordered only two countries, what would change?

There's no single right answer. The reasoning is the evidence.

### Misconception Inventory

| # | Misconception | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | France = Paris | Students conflate the capital's character with the country's — missing the sharp rural/urban divide and strong regional identities (Brittany, Alsace, Provence, Corsica). Paris dominates but does not define France geographically. |
| 2 | France is a small country | At ~551,000 km² (metropolitan), France is the largest country in the EU by area. Its overseas territories extend its footprint to every inhabited continent. Geographic size is not the same as political weight — but France has more of both than students typically assume. |
| 3 | Laïcité means France has no religion | Laïcité means the state is secular — not that religion is absent or suppressed. It is a constitutional principle governing the relationship between state and religious institutions, not a demographic fact about belief. France has significant religious communities; the state simply does not recognize or fund them. |
| 4 | The EU erased France's sovereignty | France is a founding architect of the EU and chose integration strategically. It retains a nuclear deterrent, a permanent UNSC seat, an independent foreign policy, and the capacity to veto EU decisions in its core interests. Integration was an instrument of French power, not a constraint on it. |
| 5 | France's global influence comes only from culture | The Francophone world (300M+ speakers), the world's second-largest EEZ, overseas territories on every inhabited continent, and a nuclear deterrent are structural — not cultural — sources of global reach. Culture is the most visible layer; the geographic and military architecture underneath it is what makes France's cultural influence durable. |

### MOM Rule — Remediation Routing Framing [ADDED]

Students below the mastery threshold are routed with:
**"Let's go deeper on [concept area]"** — not presented as failure or falling behind.

Concept-area routing means a student who misses climate zone questions receives
climate zone remediation; a student who misses cultural geography questions receives
cultural geography remediation. The routing message names what the student is going
deeper into — not what they got wrong. Remediation content uses the same real-world
framing (AOC, Paris, the hexagon) to maintain geographic coherence across the
re-teaching path.
