CHAPTER STRUCTURE

The chapter runs in two tiers:

  Tier 1 — Continental Frame Modules (teach the region as a system)
  Tier 2 — Country-Level Modules (apply the system to specific places)

Tier 1 must precede Tier 2. Country modules spiral back to the
continental frame concepts in every practice set.

Module 7.0 — Europe & Russia: The Geographic Frame
  Module ID:        GEO-EU-FRAME-001
  Weight:           Anchor (within chapter)
  Estimated Time:   4–5 class sessions / ~1 week
  Content Status:   Not started

  Enduring Understanding:
    Europe's outsized global influence relative to its physical size
    is not accidental — it is a product of geographic position,
    coastal access, navigable rivers, and a temperate climate zone
    that made intensive agriculture and dense settlement possible.
    Russia's scale inverts that logic: it is vast, resource-rich,
    and climatically constrained in ways that have shaped its
    political behavior for centuries.

  Topic Summary:
    The orientation module for the entire chapter. Students build
    a working mental map of the region before any country-level
    detail is introduced. Physical geography, climate distribution,
    and the Europe-Russia contrast are the three pillars. No
    political geography in this module — physical and human
    foundations only.

  Key Concepts:
    - Europe's physical extent and defining features (peninsulas,
      island nations, mountain barriers, river systems)
    - The North European Plain as a geographic corridor
    - Climate zone distribution across Europe (oceanic, continental,
      Mediterranean, subarctic)
    - Russia's physical scale — Ural Mountains as the
      Europe/Asia boundary, Siberian climate zones, permafrost
    - How physical geography explains settlement density patterns
    - Europe as a peninsula of peninsulas (Iberian, Italian,
      Scandinavian, Balkan) — why this matters for trade

  Bloom Entry / Target:   Remember → Apply
  Fink Dimensions:        Foundational Knowledge; Integration
      (connecting physical geography to settlement patterns)
  NCGE Standards:         Std 1, Std 3, Std 4, Std 7
  Prerequisites:          Chs. 1–4 (full foundation tool kit)

  Spiral Tags:
    [SPIRAL: Map Skills] — mental mapping of Europe; coordinate
    location of major physical features
    [SPIRAL: Climate Zones] — identifying climate zones from
    a map; comparing oceanic vs. continental climates
    [SPIRAL: Landforms & Physical Geography] — river systems
    as trade corridors; mountain ranges as barriers

  Sequencing Note:
    Required before all Tier 2 modules. A student who skips this
    module will lack the spatial orientation to make sense of any
    country-level content. This is the chapter's anchor.

Module 7.1 — The European Union: Geography of Cooperation
  Module ID:        GEO-EU-EU-001
  Weight:           Heavy
  Estimated Time:   4–5 sessions / ~1 week
  Content Status:   Not started

  Enduring Understanding:
    The European Union is not simply a political arrangement — it
    is a geographic response to centuries of continental conflict.
    Its member boundaries, free movement zones, and economic
    integration patterns are as much a geographic story as a
    political one.

  Topic Summary:
    The EU as a geographic system: what it is, where its members
    are, what the borders mean, and why the shape of European
    integration reflects physical and economic geography. Students
    analyze which countries joined and when — and use geography
    to explain the pattern. Brexit features as a case study in
    geographic identity.

  Key Concepts:
    - EU member geography — western core vs. eastern expansion
    - Schengen Area as a geographic border-removal system
    - The Eurozone and non-Eurozone members — spatial pattern
    - Economic geography of the EU (industrial core in Rhine
      corridor; agricultural periphery)
    - Brexit as a geographic case study — why an island nation's
      relationship to continental integration is distinctive
    - EU expansion eastward — what drove it and what resisted it

  Bloom Entry / Target:   Apply → Analyze
  Fink Dimensions:        Integration; Human Dimension
      (how political geography shapes daily life)
  NCGE Standards:         Std 5, Std 11, Std 13
  Prerequisites:          Module 7.0 (geographic frame)

  Spiral Tags:
    [SPIRAL: Map Skills] — thematic map reading; interpreting
    political boundary maps
    [SPIRAL: Human Geography Fundamentals] — economic integration,
    migration within the Schengen Area
    [SPIRAL: Landforms & Physical Geography] — Rhine corridor as
    an industrial and transport axis

  Sequencing Note:
    Placed before country modules because EU membership status
    is referenced in every Western European country profile.
    Students need the EU frame before France, Germany, or Spain
    make full sense geographically.

Module 7.2 — Russia: Scale, Climate, and Constraint
  Module ID:        GEO-EU-RU-FRAME-001
  Weight:           Heavy
  Estimated Time:   4–5 sessions / ~1 week
  Content Status:   Not started
  ⚠️ Political Sensitivity Flag: Contemporary Russia-Ukraine
     content excluded from this module. Physical and historical
     geography only until educator clearance.

  Enduring Understanding:
    Russia's geographic scale is both its defining asset and its
    defining constraint. Vast territory, immense natural resources,
    and 11 time zones coexist with brutal climate zones, limited
    warm-water port access, and a permafrost layer that covers
    roughly 65% of the land. These physical facts have shaped
    Russian strategic behavior for centuries.

  Topic Summary:
    Russia as a geographic entity — physical scale, climate
    constraints, resource geography, and population distribution.
    The module deliberately avoids contemporary geopolitical
    events and focuses on the physical and historical geographic
    logic that underlies Russian strategic priorities. Why does
    access to warm-water ports matter? Why is the population
    concentrated west of the Urals? Why is Siberia resource-rich
    but settlement-sparse? These are the questions.

  Key Concepts:
    - Russia's physical extent — world's largest country by area
    - Climate zones across Russia (subarctic dominance,
      temperate western fringe, tundra north)
    - Permafrost — what it is, where it is, why it matters
    - Natural resource geography (oil, natural gas, timber,
      minerals — distribution and significance)
    - Population distribution — why 75%+ live west of the Urals
    - Warm-water port geography as a historical strategic driver
    - Trans-Siberian Railway as a geographic solution to scale

  Bloom Entry / Target:   Apply → Analyze
  Fink Dimensions:        Foundational Knowledge; Integration
      (connecting physical geography to strategic behavior)
  NCGE Standards:         Std 4, Std 7, Std 15, Std 16, Std 17
  Prerequisites:          Module 7.0 (geographic frame)

  Spiral Tags:
    [SPIRAL: Climate Zones] — subarctic and tundra climate zones
    in depth; permafrost as a climate consequence
    [SPIRAL: Landforms & Physical Geography] — Ural Mountains as
    a continental divide; Siberian river systems
    [SPIRAL: Human Geography Fundamentals] — resource-driven
    settlement patterns; internal migration logic

  Sequencing Note:
    Placed after the EU module to establish the Europe-Russia
    contrast structurally. Students should understand European
    integration before encountering Russia as an alternative
    geographic and political model. Country-level module for
    Russia deferred — physical frame here, country detail later
    if curriculum expands.

Module 7.3 — France: Geography at Europe's Crossroads
  Module ID:        GEO-EU-FR-001
  Weight:           Heavy
  Estimated Time:   5–6 sessions / ~1.5 weeks
  Content Status:   Alpha — spec complete (GEO-EU-FR-001.docx)
                    Diagnostic feedback and practice questions
                    not yet drafted.

  Enduring Understanding:
    France's geographic position at Western Europe's crossroads —
    between Atlantic and Mediterranean, between sea and Alps —
    has shaped its role as a cultural exporter, agricultural
    powerhouse, and political architect of the modern European
    order. Geography doesn't determine history, but France is a
    clear case where it heavily pressures it.

  Key Concepts (summary — full spec in GEO-EU-FR-001):
    - Physical geography (Alps, Pyrenees, Loire, Seine, Rhône)
    - Three climate zones within one country
    - France as a primate city / Paris dominance pattern
    - AOC system as geography encoded in law
    - France's global reach (EEZ, Francophonie, overseas territories)
    - Franco-German relationship as the spine of the EU

  Bloom Entry / Target:   Apply → Evaluate
  NCGE Standards:         Std 3, Std 4, Std 7, Std 9, Std 11,
                          Std 13, Std 17, Std 18
  Prerequisites:          Modules 7.0 + 7.1 (geographic and EU frame)

  Spiral Tags (from module spec):
    [SPIRAL: Map Skills] [SPIRAL: Climate & Weather Systems]
    [SPIRAL: Landforms & Physical Geography]
    [SPIRAL: Human Geography Fundamentals]

  Sequencing Note:
    First country module in the chapter. Placed here because the
    full module spec exists and because France's geographic
    complexity — three climate zones, eight land borders, two
    coastlines, overseas territories on every continent — makes
    it an exceptionally rich first case study.

Module 7.4 — Germany: The Industrial Core
  Module ID:        GEO-EU-DE-001
  Weight:           Moderate
  Estimated Time:   4–5 sessions / ~1 week
  Content Status:   Not started

  Enduring Understanding:
    Germany's central position on the North European Plain, its
    river network (Rhine, Elbe, Danube), and its lack of natural
    barriers have made it both the economic engine of the EU and
    historically the most contested territory in European politics.
    Geography explains both its industrial dominance and its
    geopolitical vulnerability.

  Key Concepts:
    - Physical geography — North European Plain, Rhine corridor,
      Black Forest, Bavarian Alps
    - Industrial geography — Ruhr Valley as manufacturing core;
      post-industrial transition
    - Reunification geography — East vs. West economic divide
      (still visible in spatial data 35 years later)
    - Germany as the EU's largest economy — trade geography
    - Population geography — aging, immigration, regional
      economic disparity

  Bloom Entry / Target:   Apply → Analyze
  NCGE Standards:         Std 4, Std 11, Std 12, Std 17
  Prerequisites:          Modules 7.0, 7.1, 7.3 (France provides
                          the cross-channel comparison point)

  Spiral Tags:
    [SPIRAL: Map Skills] [SPIRAL: Landforms & Physical Geography]
    [SPIRAL: Human Geography Fundamentals]
    [SPIRAL: Climate Zones]

  Sequencing Note:
    France-Germany pairing is deliberate. The Franco-German
    relationship is the political spine of the EU — students
    who have studied France will read Germany's geography in
    direct dialogue with it.

Module 7.5 — The United Kingdom: An Island Apart
  Module ID:        GEO-EU-UK-001
  Weight:           Moderate
  Estimated Time:   3–4 sessions
  Content Status:   Not started

  Enduring Understanding:
    Island geography is not just a physical fact — it is a political
    identity. The UK's separation from the European continent by
    22 miles of water (the English Channel at its narrowest) has
    shaped its strategic choices for over a millennium, including
    Brexit. The question isn't whether island geography determines
    British behavior — it's how much pressure it applies.

  Key Concepts:
    - UK physical geography — island nation structure, highland
      zones (Scotland, Wales), Thames estuary
    - Maritime geography and its economic legacy
    - Brexit as a geographic case study — island identity vs.
      continental integration
    - UK as a devolved union — England, Scotland, Wales, N. Ireland
      as geographic and political units
    - Post-industrial geography — former manufacturing regions
      vs. London's financial dominance

  Bloom Entry / Target:   Analyze → Evaluate
  NCGE Standards:         Std 4, Std 5, Std 13, Std 17
  Prerequisites:          Modules 7.0, 7.1 (EU frame essential —
                          UK is partly defined by its relationship
                          to EU geography)

  Spiral Tags:
    [SPIRAL: Map Skills] [SPIRAL: Human Geography Fundamentals]
    [SPIRAL: Climate Zones]

  Sequencing Note:
    Placed after France and Germany so students approach Brexit
    analytically — they understand what the UK opted out of
    before evaluating the geographic logic of that choice.

Module 7.6 — The Iberian Peninsula: Spain & Portugal
  Module ID:        GEO-EU-IB-001
  Weight:           Moderate
  Estimated Time:   3–4 sessions
  Content Status:   Not started

  Enduring Understanding:
    The Iberian Peninsula is physically isolated from the rest of
    Europe by the Pyrenees and geographically oriented toward the
    Atlantic — a fact that explains why Spain and Portugal became
    the world's first maritime empires rather than continental
    powers. Geography pointed them outward when the land route
    east was controlled by others.

  Key Concepts:
    - Iberian physical geography — Meseta plateau, Pyrenees
      barrier, Atlantic and Mediterranean coastlines
    - Climate zones within the peninsula (Mediterranean, semi-arid,
      oceanic northwest)
    - Atlantic maritime orientation and the age of exploration
    - Spain's regional diversity — Catalonia, Basque Country,
      Andalusia as geographic and cultural units
    - Portugal's global legacy via the Lusophone world
    - Tourism geography — Costa del Sol, Barcelona,
      Algarve as economic geography

  Bloom Entry / Target:   Analyze → Evaluate
  NCGE Standards:         Std 4, Std 7, Std 11, Std 17
  Prerequisites:          Modules 7.0, 7.3 (France establishes
                          the Pyrenees as a shared boundary)

  Spiral Tags:
    [SPIRAL: Climate Zones] [SPIRAL: Landforms & Physical Geography]
    [SPIRAL: Human Geography Fundamentals]
    [SPIRAL: Map Skills]

  Sequencing Note:
    Placed after the core Western European modules. The Atlantic
    orientation theme connects back to Latin America (Ch. 6) —
    the first explicit cross-chapter spiral connection.
